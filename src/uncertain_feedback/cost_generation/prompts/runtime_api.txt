Runtime API:
- You write LIMITS, not costs. Define one or both of exactly these functions:
    def path_margins(features, params):
    def speed_margins(features, params):
  Each returns a dict mapping a short name to one MARGIN per limit: a torch
  tensor of shape (n_rollouts, horizon), in physical units, POSITIVE where the
  limit is violated and zero or negative where it is met. "Keep x at least t"
  is the margin `t - x`; "keep x at most t" is `x - t`; a band is two margins.
  Never clamp, square, weight, sum or average a margin, and never return a
  penalty: the system turns each margin into its own penalty. The planner
  treats a limit as a wall (it charges the worst timestep's excess, and only
  the excess beyond what the arm already has where it starts); the motion
  generator is steered by a smooth version of the same margins.
- path_margins says where the arm may be. Its features maps each joint-feature
  name to a torch tensor of shape (n_rollouts, horizon). Values are radians, and
  so are its margins:
  features["elbow_flexion"]
  features["shoulder_flexion_extension"]
  features["shoulder_abduction_adduction"]
  features["shoulder_elevation"]
  features["shoulder_internal_external_rotation"]
  The shoulder features are preference-friendly component-angle approximations
  in the spine3 frame: flexion/extension is signed upper-arm movement along
  the z axis, abduction/adduction is signed lateral upper-arm movement along
  the x axis (positive is away from the torso for the left arm), and
  internal/external rotation is the signed swing-twist of the anatomical
  shoulder rotation about the T-pose upper-arm axis. Clavicle rotation does not
  contribute. Positive = internal rotation, twisting the forearm toward the
  body midline; negative = external rotation.
  shoulder_elevation is total upper-arm elevation from straight down (0 = arm
  hanging at side, pi/2 = horizontal, pi = overhead); it increases when the arm
  is raised in any direction and is the only feature that can express a height
  limit independent of whether the arm was raised forward or sideways.
- speed_margins says how fast the arm may be moved. Define it ONLY when the
  instruction is about speed. Its features holds the joint features above and,
  measured over the step into each timestep (20 timesteps per second):
  features["wrist_speed"]   speed of the hand relative to the torso, m/s, >= 0
  features["elbow_speed"]   speed of the elbow relative to the torso, m/s, >= 0
  features["elbow_flexion_rate"]
  features["shoulder_flexion_extension_rate"]
  features["shoulder_abduction_adduction_rate"]
  features["shoulder_elevation_rate"]
  features["shoulder_internal_external_rotation_rate"]
  A rate is the signed change of its joint feature in rad/s, positive while the
  feature increases: elbow_flexion_rate is positive while the elbow bends and
  negative while it straightens, so `-features["elbow_flexion_rate"] - cap`
  limits how fast the elbow is straightened and leaves bending free. Its
  margins are in m/s or rad/s. A cap may depend on the pose (through interp of
  a joint feature) but must stay strictly positive everywhere, so that moving
  slowly enough always satisfies it.
- torch is available, and so is interp(x, xp, fp): np.interp for a tensor x,
  linear between the anchor points (xp, fp) and holding the end values outside
  them. Do not import anything.
- The same margins also steer motion generation by gradient, so they must be
  differentiable in the features. Never turn a comparison into a boolean, count
  or mask, never branch on tensor values, and never call .item() or .numpy().
- If you need statistics such as the elbow_flexion range of the chosen
  trajectory, read those values from the Summaries section below and encode
  them as hardcoded floats or params.
- Every margin must be a finite torch tensor with shape (n_rollouts, horizon).
