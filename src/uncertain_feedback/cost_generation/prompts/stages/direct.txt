The limits must order the motions as the person did: the chosen correction's worst step must go past them by strictly less than the original plan's and each marked-wrong candidate's (it need not stay inside them entirely), and the arm must still be able to reach the goal under them. The robot treats each limit as a wall.

Limits are one or a few bounds on the joint features. A bound can be:
- one-sided: a minimum or a maximum on a feature;
- two-sided: a [low, high] range on a feature (two margins);
- pose-dependent: a minimum or maximum on one feature whose threshold moves with another feature, computed per timestep with interp(other feature, anchor values, thresholds);
- a speed cap, only when the instruction is about speed: a maximum on wrist_speed, elbow_speed or one direction of a joint feature's rate, constant or moving with a joint feature, and always above zero.
Different bounds may limit different features. A marked-wrong candidate may have been wrong on a different feature than the one the instruction names.
Use a pose-dependent bound only when the instruction itself relates two joints (e.g. "the higher you lift my arm, the more my elbow should bend") or a speed to a posture (e.g. "slower when my elbow is nearly straight"). One correction shows the preference at one pose, so a relationship seen only in these trajectories is not evidence of one; use constant thresholds instead.
