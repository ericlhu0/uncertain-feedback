Hard requirements:
- State every limit over the features in `features`. Path limits are joint-angle limits (radians).
- Do NOT use Cartesian or world-space positions. Position data in the Summaries is for understanding the correction only — translate any "reach up", "lower", or "across the body" intent into the corresponding joint-angle feature(s) above, not into wrist/elbow x/y/z heights or points. The only Cartesian quantities you may limit are the two speeds, wrist_speed and elbow_speed, and only inside speed_margins.
- Use speed features ONLY when the instruction is about speed (too fast, slow down, more gently, not so quickly, ...): then define speed_margins. Otherwise define path_margins alone. Never add a speed, smoothness or acceleration limit the person did not ask for.
- Keep path limits and speed limits apart: path_margins never reads a speed feature, and each speed limit is its own margin in speed_margins.
- Write no weights and no penalties. Params hold thresholds, caps and anchor points only.
- Return only JSON with keys: description, code, params, explanation, recipient_explanation.
- Avoid exact joint-pose matching by default. Prefer one-sided limits or a tolerance band on a joint-feature angle (two margins). Use an exact joint-angle target only when the instruction clearly calls for a precise posture.
- Use the least number of limits possible. If one limit explains the realistic human preference, use one.
- Params should usually contain no more than three values; if more are needed, simplify the limits. A pose-dependent limit may use a few extra values for its conditioning anchor points.
- The description field must state the caregiver preference the limits encode in plain human language — how a caregiver would explain what they are trying to achieve. Do not describe the code structure or mathematical approach.
- The explanation field must describe in 3–6 sentences: (1) what preferences the limits capture and why they are implied by the instruction and trajectory, (2) which limits were chosen and what each one rules out, and (3) which specific numbers were drawn from the trajectory data and where they came from (e.g. "the elbow-flexion limit of 1.1 rad came from the median elbow_flexion of the MDM joint-feature range"). This field is for developer understanding — be specific and concrete.
- The recipient_explanation field must be 1–3 brief, plain-language sentences you would say directly to the care recipient. Avoid technical terms, measurements, code, "cost", "penalty", "margin", and robot-control jargon. Explain in layman terms how their arm will now be moved; they should understand what motions their arm will and won't be taken through.
