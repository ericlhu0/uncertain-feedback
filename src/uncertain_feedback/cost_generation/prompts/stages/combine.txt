You are unifying {round_count} sets of limits learned from separate corrections given by the
same care recipient at different arm poses. Produce ONE set of limits that explains every
round. It replaces all per-round limits; never stack them.

Use these joint features:
- `elbow_flexion`: 0 is straight; larger values are more bent.
- `shoulder_abduction_adduction`: positive is away from the torso; negative is across it.
- `shoulder_flexion_extension`: positive is toward +z; negative is toward -z.
- `shoulder_elevation`: 0 is down at the side, pi/2 is horizontal, pi is overhead.
- `shoulder_internal_external_rotation`: positive is internal rotation; negative is external.
and, only for a round whose instruction was about speed, the speed features: `wrist_speed`
and `elbow_speed` (m/s, relative to the torso) and the signed `<joint feature>_rate` of each
feature above (rad/s, positive while the feature increases).

Each per-round set of limits is a PROVISIONAL local explanation of one correction at one pose,
not an established global preference. Your task is to model this person's arm mobility
limits from all rounds together. Before choosing a form, consult the biomechanics and
clinical literature on human arm mobility limits (you have web search): for example how
one joint's range depends on the position of others, what limits and painful ranges are
typical of conditions that match what the rounds show, how the speed a joint tolerates
depends on its angle, and how discomfort grows toward a limit. Use what you find to propose
hypotheses; the rounds decide between them. A path limit may be any differentiable function of the five features, of
whatever shape the evidence supports; a speed limit any differentiable cap that stays above
zero. A pattern from the literature that the rounds do not support stays out of the
limits. In "explanation", name the source behind each limit and the rounds that
support it.

The feedback wording may be identical in every round because simulated personas use a
canned line. Infer pose variation from trigger poses, trajectories, numeric summaries,
and images rather than repeated wording. Open every listed image before authoring.

Two kinds of motion constrain the unified limits differently.
- ACCEPTED motion is what was executed without complaint: each round's executed history
  (`human.history` in its eval state), and the per-frame features of the
  executed-trajectory corpus when it is provided. It must stay inside every limit, frame
  by frame, speeds included. Verify against the ACTUAL poses visited — summary ranges
  cannot validate a limit that depends on several features. If any accepted frame is
  past a limit, relax the limit there and re-check every round.
- A CHOSEN CORRECTION (`correction_traj`) was only PREFERRED to what it replaced: the plan

  plan and each marked-wrong candidate it was chosen over. The render command prints this
  ordering per round; a round where it fails is a round the limits contradict.
Nothing else is evidence: no motion after a correction was shown to the recipient. A round
deleted during the run kept the arm from its goal: its preference is real, but the unified
limits must be looser there. Every round's goal must stay reachable.
