Hard requirements:
- Use ONLY joint-space preferences. Build the cost exclusively from the named joint-feature helpers: context.elbow_flexion_angles, context.shoulder_flexion_extension_angles, context.shoulder_abduction_adduction_angles, context.shoulder_elevation_angles, and context.shoulder_internal_external_rotation_angles. All terms must be expressed in joint-angle space (radians). Do not infer anatomical features from raw axis-angle components.
- Do NOT use Cartesian or world-space positions in the cost. Do not call context.fk_rollouts or context.fk_batch, do not read context.mdm_positions / context.current_positions / context.recent_positions or any *_positions array, and do not use context.joint_index. Position/FK data and the Summaries are for understanding the correction only — translate any "reach up", "lower", or "across the body" intent into the corresponding joint-angle feature(s) above, not into wrist/elbow x/y/z heights or points.
- Return only JSON with keys: description, code, params, explanation, recipient_explanation.
- Prefer costs over future timesteps q_trajs[:, 1:], not only the initial state.
- Do not include smoothness, velocity, or acceleration costs.
- Avoid exact joint-pose matching by default. Prefer one-sided bounds or a tolerance band on a joint-feature angle, penalizing only deviations beyond it. Use an exact joint-angle target only when the instruction clearly calls for a precise posture.
- Use the least number of cost terms possible. If one term explains the realistic human preference, use one term.
- Params should usually contain no more than three values; if more are needed, simplify the cost. A pose-dependent bound may use a few extra values for its conditioning anchor points.
- The description field must state the caregiver preference the cost encodes in plain human language — how a caregiver would explain what they are trying to achieve. Do not describe the code structure or mathematical approach.
- The explanation field must describe in 3–6 sentences: (1) what preferences the cost captures and why they are implied by the instruction and trajectory, (2) which cost terms were chosen and what each one penalizes, and (3) which specific numbers were drawn from the trajectory data and where they came from (e.g. "the elbow-flexion bound of 1.1 rad came from the median elbow_flexion of the MDM joint-feature range"). This field is for developer understanding — be specific and concrete.
- The recipient_explanation field must be 1–3 brief, plain-language sentences you would say directly to the care recipient. Avoid technical terms, measurements, code, "cost", "penalty", and robot-control jargon. Explain how the new cost functions will guide their arm movement in layman terms; they should be understand what motions their arms will and won't be able to make with the cost functions.
